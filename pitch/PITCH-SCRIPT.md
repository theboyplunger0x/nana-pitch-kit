# Nana pitch script — current 16-slide deck

Target length: approximately 5–6 minutes.

This is a narrative baseline, not a script that must be memorized word for word.

## 01 — Cover

Nana gives people financial independence through voice, with protection when it actually matters. We are starting with seniors and people with limited mobility—people for whom the problem is not understanding money, but interacting with the technology around it.

## 02 — The problem

As my grandmothers got older, our way of protecting them slowly became doing things for them: paying bills, moving money, making decisions. One of them was also the victim of a financial scam, so the risk was real. But the tradeoff was real too. Protection started becoming control.

Today families choose between taking over or leaving someone exposed. Nana creates a third option: act independently and escalate only when necessary.

## 03 — Why now

This is a structural problem. The population is ageing, disability already affects more than a billion people, and financial life keeps moving toward screens, passwords and increasingly complex interfaces. Accessibility cannot be another settings menu. It has to reshape the product.

## 04 — The product

With Nana, the user says what they want in natural language. The agent resolves the intent, prepares the action and shows only what matters. The human confirms the consequence, and Nana executes.

The agent handles complexity. The human retains meaningful control.

## 05 — Two users

We discovered that Nana has two users. The end user wants independence. The responsible person—a son, daughter or trusted family member—wants peace of mind without becoming somebody else's financial interface.

Those are distinct roles inside one trusted relationship.

## 06 — The principle

The most important rule is that the responsible person intervenes by exception, not by default. If every transaction needs family approval, we did not restore independence. We just digitized dependency.

## 07 — Protection engine

For an ordinary action, Nana prepares, explains, confirms and executes. For something unusual—a new recipient, a strange amount or a pattern outside policy—Nana pauses and escalates.

Protection becomes an exception engine rather than parental control.

## 08 — Delegated execution

The technical challenge is allowing the agent to act without giving it unrestricted control. We are exploring smart accounts, programmable permissions, delegated execution and multisig escalation.

The user keeps authority. The agent receives permissions that are bounded, transparent and revocable.

## 09 — Proof

This is more than a conversational mockup. The first prototype used Tether WDK and completed seven confirmed transactions on Ethereum Sepolia. Nana won second place in the WDK track at Aleph.

The hackathon did not prove product-market fit, but it proved the core agent-to-wallet loop can work.

## 10 — What Nana owns

Nana owns the experience people trust: the agent, voice UX, Protection Engine, Family Graph, policies, orchestration, brand and user relationship.

Wallet infrastructure, stablecoins, KYC and local rails can come from partners. WDK and USDT are the path we already proved. Circle and Arc are another path we are evaluating. The product should remain independent from any single rail.

## 11 — Business model

We have three monetization hypotheses: revenue from financial activity, Nana Family for expanded family coordination, and eventually B2B2C distribution or licensing.

One rule is already clear: core safety stays free. Nana never charges someone simply to be protected. Nana Family monetizes complexity and coordination across a larger family network.

## 12 — Expansion

We start where the need is strongest: seniors and people with limited mobility. The same primitive can expand into family finance and eventually delegated finance more broadly.

The architecture can be horizontal without making the initial go-to-market broad.

## 13 — Go to market

Our current hypothesis is that the responsible person opens the door. A daughter or son discovers Nana, helps with setup and trusted relationships, and then steps back. The end user owns the account, speaks, confirms and keeps authority.

Acquisition by trust. Retention by independence.

## 14 — What exists and what comes next

We already have the working product core: voice intent, agent-prepared transfer, explicit confirmation, receipt and the dual-user protection concept.

Next we need to validate the hardest assumptions: responsible-led acquisition, Family Graph configuration, escalation thresholds, willingness to pay and the right infrastructure path by market.

## 15 — Team

We are four builders working across product, mobile, agent flows, wallet infrastructure and voice. Nana was discovered and built together, in the open.

## 16 — Close

We believe the next financial account is agentic: an account that lets people act independently inside a trusted, programmable network of relationships and permissions.

Independence through voice. Protection when it matters.

## Short version — 60 seconds

Nana is a voice-first agentic financial account for seniors and people with limited mobility.

Today, when someone begins to need help with financial technology, protection often means taking control away. Nana creates another option. A person expresses intent naturally—“send $20 to my grandson”—the agent prepares and explains the action, and the user confirms what matters.

Normal activity stays between the user and Nana. If an action is unusual or crosses a policy, Nana pauses and escalates to a trusted responsible person. The responsible intervenes by exception, not by default.

We built the first working loop with Tether WDK, executed seven confirmed test transactions and won second place in the WDK track at Aleph. We are now validating the dual-user product, Family Graph and business model.

Nana's long-term primitive is an agentic financial account that provides independence inside trusted, programmable relationships.
