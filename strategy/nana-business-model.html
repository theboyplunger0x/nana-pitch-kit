Nana Wallet

A consumer financial product with a dual-user model

Independence for the person using it. Protection for the person who cares for them.

Business model working document · August 2026

The thesis

Nana is not "a wallet for the elderly". That framing hides the mechanism that makes it work.

The definition

Nana is a free agentic financial account with a dual-user model: independence for the person using it, and protection for the person who cares for them.

Three roles have to be told apart, because confusing them is what makes every other question unanswerable.

The central insight

The responsible absorbs the complexity once, so the user gets simplicity every day.

And the rule that keeps it a product

The responsible must not approve every payment. The moment they do, independence was not returned, it was transferred. Intervention happens by exception only. This single rule is what separates Nana from parental controls, and it should survive every future product decision.

Why this ordering matters commercially

KYC, funding and configuration are where consumer fintech loses most of its funnel. Moving all three onto a motivated, capable buyer changes the conversion math of the entire product, before a single feature is built.

What stays invisible

CBU, stablecoin, network, gas, on and off ramp, address. None of it reaches the person paying. The agent does the work; the user makes the one decision that actually carries consequence.

The load-bearing rule, restated

Normal activity: user → Nana. Unusual activity only: user → Nana → responsible.

Where the liability sits, and how it is contained

Any product that says it detects scams carries exposure when it misses one. That exposure is smaller when protection is a free, universal property of the account rather than a paid promise. Charging for safety converts a best-effort feature into a purchased guarantee, which is a materially worse legal position. Keeping the safety floor free is the containment strategy, not a giveaway.

Three monetization hypotheses

Not one business model. Three bets, to be validated in sequence rather than launched together.

Layer 1 · The wallet is free, activity is monetized

Nana is downloaded and used for free. Usage generates volume through a financial partner, and Nana takes a share of the fee or spread rather than charging the senior a visible percentage.

The number this layer has to survive

Older adults are, by definition, low-volume users. Ten payments of $20,000 ARS per month is roughly USD 150 of monthly volume. At a 1% spread split evenly with the partner, that is well under one dollar per user per month. Layer 1 is presented first but may be the weakest of the three. Either the volume assumption is wrong, or the sequencing is, and knowing which one is a research question, not an opinion.

Layer 2 · Nana Family, sold on capability

The line that must not be crossed

Safety is never the upsell. Every Nana account, paid or not, gets the confirmation step, the limits and the anomaly detection. The subscription sells reach, control and convenience, never protection itself.

Charging for protection would mean the free product knowingly leaves someone exposed. That is bad to sell, bad to defend, and it manufactures the exact liability the company should be avoiding. The paid tier has to earn its price on capability instead.

The pitch is not pay to keep your mother safe. It is closer to you are looking after two parents, from another city, with your sibling. Here is the account that handles that. A caregiving household with real coordination needs is a different customer from one adult child with one parent, and only the first one has a reason to pay.

What still has to be proven

Willingness to pay is asserted, not measured. The honest test is narrow: does a household with more than one caregiver, or more than one dependent, hit a wall in the free tier that they want removed? If the free tier is enough for almost everyone, this layer does not exist and the answer is layer 1 or layer 3.

Layer 3 · B2B2C, the largest and least certain

Rather than acquiring every older adult individually, Nana becomes a layer an institution offers: Nana Mode, powered by [bank]. The bank already has users, KYC, ARS accounts, cards, QR and compliance. Nana contributes the voice agent, the accessibility, the family relationships, the protection policies and the agentic execution. Priced per active user, as an enterprise licence, or as revenue share.

The assumption underneath

That banks want this. They carry accessibility obligations but modest commercial incentive. This is the most speculative of the three and should be labelled that way in any conversation.

This ordering is a choice, not a dependency

Nothing forces layer 1 to come first. Willingness to pay can be tested with a priced fake door in a week, without building a single rail. The sequence above optimises for what is easiest to build; a sequence optimised for what is riskiest to assume would test pricing first. Worth deciding on purpose.

The positioning that follows from it

Not "are you 78? download our crypto wallet". Instead: give your parents financial independence without giving up your peace of mind.

The loop worth testing

The responsible downloads Nana and invites a parent. The parent uses it and adds family recipients. Those relatives see Nana working inside their own family. Some of them become the responsible in another household.

Whether this behaves as a real growth loop is unknown. It is cheap to instrument and worth measuring early.

Missing from this document, and needed before any investor conversation

Regulation. ARS on and off ramp, stablecoin custody, third-party funds, and a vulnerable-adult user base. Argentina's PSAV regime and the compliance path are absent here, and they shape which of the three layers is even legal to run alone.

What to say in the room

The user architecture and the go to market are defined. The revenue model is three hypotheses, not a conclusion. Validate who adopts, who uses and who pays, and the revenue question stops being speculative.
